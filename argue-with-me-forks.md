# Argue With Me — the six forks

Exactly the text now live in `script.js`. Mark up anything you want changed.

**What changed from the last pass, from your notes:** the question is now a
general product question anyone can answer cold. No project name, no project
detail, nothing the reader needs context for. The head of the card names the
kind of decision instead of the product. The product appears only in the reveal,
as the first line, followed by the three lines.

So the order is: topic → question → two options → *pick* → what I did, and why.

## Fork 1

**Card head:** Who you build for · reach against depth

**Question:** You can serve the biggest group of users, or the smaller group with the sharpest version of the problem. Which one does v1 go to?

**Note under it:** The small group is a fraction of the market. The big one is most of it.

- **Option A:** The smaller group, where the problem bites hardest ← *my call*
- **Option B:** The bigger group, where the traffic already is

**Reveal**

> **FitCheck. I aimed the extension at people shopping on a laptop.**
>
> **What I knew.** Mobile is roughly 60% of fashion traffic in India, and a browser extension cannot reach any of it.
>
> **What I assumed.** Fit doubt peaks on considered buys, and those get studied on a big screen with tabs open.
>
> **What would have proved me wrong.** Desktop sessions turning out as quick and single-tab as mobile ones. Then the wedge holds nobody worth serving.

Source: `fitchecker/docs/PM_DECISIONS.md` — FC.4

## Fork 2

**Card head:** What you hand them · accuracy against a read

**Question:** The data your user needs is already on the screen, and they still get the decision wrong. Do you make the data better, or change the way it is read?

**Note under it:** Better data is easy to measure. A better read is not.

- **Option A:** Make the data better. Give them the exact numbers.
- **Option B:** Change the read. Same data, shown so they can act on it. ← *my call*

**Reveal**

> **FitCheck. I show a tinted silhouette that reads snug, regular or loose.**
>
> **What I knew.** Every product page already carries a size chart. Accuracy was not the thing missing.
>
> **What I assumed.** People cannot turn 36 inches into how a cut will sit on them. They want a read, not a sum.
>
> **What would have proved me wrong.** Shoppers glancing at the silhouette and going back to the chart anyway. Then the gap was arithmetic after all.

Source: `fitchecker/docs/PM_DECISIONS.md` — FC.1

## Fork 3

**Card head:** When you speak · in the moment against after it

**Question:** You can step in while someone is deciding, or wait until they can see the whole picture. When do you help?
**Note under it:** One is the obvious moment. The other is the one they still open in month three.

- **Option A:** While they are deciding, when it can still change the outcome
- **Option B:** Afterwards, when the whole picture is in front of them ← *my call*

**Reveal**

> **Savio. It speaks at the end of the month, not at the checkout.**
>
> **What I knew.** Apps that ping at the moment of purchase get muted. That is the churn story across the category.
>
> **What I assumed.** By the time the app pings, the person has already decided. The nudge arrives after the commitment.
>
> **What would have proved me wrong.** A month of reflection changing nothing in the month that follows. Then the timing was mine and not theirs.

Source: `Savio/PM_DECISIONS.md` — Core thesis

## Fork 4

**Card head:** When they fall short · recovery against a clean slate

**Question:** A user misses the target your product set with them. Do you offer a way to make it up, or clear the slate and start again?

**Note under it:** Both are honest. Only one of them gets opened again.

- **Option A:** Clear the slate, and say plainly what happened ← *my call*
- **Option B:** Offer a plan to make up the shortfall

**Reveal**

> **Savio. A month that closes short is stated, and the next one starts clean.**
>
> **What I knew.** Short months are normal at ₹40K to ₹1.2L a month. That is the segment, not an edge case.
>
> **What I assumed.** A payback screen reads as punishment, and people stop opening the app instead of paying it back.
>
> **What would have proved me wrong.** Users finishing a payback plan and coming back the next month. Then shame was a working mechanic and I cut it for nothing.

Source: `Savio/PM_DECISIONS.md` — Rollover model, decision 4

## Fork 5

**Card head:** What you ask for · evidence against momentum

**Question:** Your product finds a real problem but cannot size it. Do you tell the team what to fix, or what to go and measure?

**Note under it:** One is the answer they wanted. The other is the one your evidence can carry.

- **Option A:** What to measure. Name the number that would settle it. ← *my call*
- **Option B:** What to fix. Name the change so they can start on Monday.

**Reveal**

> **Amazon Discovery. Each weekly digest ends with a number to go and get.**
>
> **What I knew.** Reviews can establish that something is wrong. They cannot establish how often it happens.
>
> **What I assumed.** A PM will run a one-day query this week and defer a sprint. The smaller ask is the one that moves.
>
> **What would have proved me wrong.** PMs skipping the query and acting only when told what to build. Then I handed them homework instead of a decision.

Source: `amazon-discovery-intel-repo/DECISIONS.md` — 2026-08-17, the action names a metric, an owner and a price

## Fork 6

**Card head:** What a report covers · current against complete

**Question:** Your weekly report can cover the week that just ended, or the week in progress. Which one do you send?

**Note under it:** The finished week is already a few days old. The week in progress is not finished.

- **Option A:** The week in progress, so the number is as current as it can be
- **Option B:** The week that ended, so the number is whole ← *my call*

**Reveal**

> **Amazon Discovery. The digest reports the last week that has finished.**
>
> **What I knew.** The pipeline runs at 09:00 on Monday. The newest week it could name is nine hours old and nearly empty.
>
> **What I assumed.** A reader checks the week on the label against a calendar, and one label they can disprove costs trust in every number under it.
>
> **What would have proved me wrong.** Readers wanting a running total more than a week they could verify. Then the older label was the wrong trade.

Source: `amazon-discovery-intel-repo/DECISIONS.md` — 2026-08-24, a digest is about a week that has finished

## Section copy around the deck

**Heading:** Argue With Me

**Sub:** Pick a side, and I'll show you what I knew, what I assumed on top of it, and what would have proved me wrong. *None of them had complete data. That is the job.*

**After you pick:** Same call · We differ here

The two chosen cards are now marked harder. Both get a thin inner ring, and the
card nobody picked fades back. Your pick rings in ink and is tagged *You*. My
call rings in the accent colour and is tagged *What I did*. Land on the same card
and it wears both. No red, no green — the reader is not being marked.

**Closing card:** That is how I decide.
Six calls, none of them made on complete data. Where you went the other way, I want the reasoning. That is the conversation I am after.

**Buttons:** Tell me where I got it wrong · Read them again
