ProblemA PM cannot read every review, so they skim. Skimming feels fine because you never find out what you missed. The complaint that mattered was in there, and nobody counted it.
DecisionThirteen steps, and AI runs at three of them. It reads the messy text and groups the complaints, which is what it is genuinely good at. Every number a PM then sees is worked out in code.

How it works

  1. It gathers the complaints. Play Store, App Store and Amazon listings. Play Store is the one that reliably delivers; the others chip in when they can.
  2. Sample or live, never mixed. A switch at the top decides whether you are looking at a fixed test set or reviews pulled this week. The two never blend, so a demo can never be mistaken for real data.
  3. Then it ranks. How many people hit it, how badly, how many sources agree, whether a recent version made it worse, how hard it looks to fix, and whether it is trending up or down. Formally: (reach × impact × confidence × version) / effort × trend.

The calls I made

  • AI reads, code ranks. Sorting thousands of messy complaints into themes is exactly what a model is good at, so that is its job here. Turning those themes into a priority order is arithmetic, and arithmetic is what a PM has to defend in a roadmap meeting.
  • Nothing disappears quietly. If a review is dropped during the clean, the digest says how many went and why. 140 in, 136 kept.
  • Every claim is traceable. Ask the digest a question and each sentence in the answer footnotes the review it came from.
