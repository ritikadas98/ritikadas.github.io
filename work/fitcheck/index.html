ProblemFree returns are why people in India buy clothes online. You can't tell if something fits, so you order it and send it back. A quarter to a third of orders come back. Size is the biggest reason. Each one costs the retailer shipping both ways, handling, and often a garment it can't sell again. The chart that would prevent it sits on the page, unread.

How it works

  1. It reads the page. Each of the three stores publishes its size chart in a different way, so there is a small reader for each one. They all hand back the same set of numbers.
  2. It does the maths. Your body against what the garment was actually cut for. Two inches of room means slim. Eight means oversized.
  3. It looks at what you already own. On a past order it knows the size you bought, and can build your profile from it — so you never have to measure yourself.

The calls I made

  • Exact, not estimated. The store already publishes the numbers, so the fit is worked out straight from them. A centimetre of drift here costs someone a return and a reshipment, so nothing about the answer is approximated.
  • Nothing leaves your browser. Your measurements stay on your own machine. No account, no server, nothing sent anywhere.
  • It tells you when it can't tell. If the chart is missing, incomplete or published as a picture, it says so rather than inventing an answer. Eight defined cases where it does that.
